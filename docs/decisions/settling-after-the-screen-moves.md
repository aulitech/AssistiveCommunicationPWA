# Settling after the screen moves

> One of the decisions behind Peri, moved out of [AGENTS.md](../../AGENTS.md), which keeps the map of
> the source and the rules that hold everywhere. What is written here is why the code is the shape it is.

**A pointer rests where it last fired.** Whatever arrives underneath gets a `pointerenter` of its own — the browser's doing, not a mistake — so a control that appears under a resting pointer starts dwelling on nobody's instruction. The menu has guarded its own items against this from the start (`REOPEN_GUARD_MS`); `holdDwells()` is the same second, applied where the *whole screen* changes.

- **Two callers, both chosen for being where the screen moves rather than where a control fires.** `PanelBack`, which replaces the panel with whatever is behind it — and the way out of the menu lands on the board, where what is underneath is a phrase that would be spoken. And the `zoom` effect in `App.tsx`, because changing the text size relays out every control on screen around a pointer that has not moved. In `App.tsx` rather than in the settings row, so every route to a new text size is covered: the spinner, its revert, a restored backup, a board arriving from another device. Not on the first run, or the app would refuse to answer for its first second.
- **A tap is guarded exactly as a dwell is, and the keyboard is not.** That is not obvious and it is the whole point: a gaze rig can be set to send a real left click where it rests, so on the device this app is for the click *is* the dwell — the operating system is doing the holding — and a guard the platform walks around is not a guard. It cost a session's debugging to learn, from a report of the board selecting the cell under the pointer after a live re-sort that looked exactly like the guard failing. What it costs in return is a mouse user losing a click in the second after a panel closes or the text size changes, which is the same second their hover dwell is already deaf for. Enter and Space are left alone, because they are aimed by focus rather than by position and focus follows its element when the screen moves. `screenJustMoved` is the single question both routes ask.
- **It stops controls arming and leaves a control already being held alone.** That line is the whole design. Somebody resting on a control is deliberately working it — the text-size spinner is exactly that, and cancelling it would mean a hold that stepped the size once and stopped rather than repeating. What the guard is for is the opposite case, and every one of those arrives through `start`.
- **Once the second passes, a pointer still resting on something stays inert until it moves.** Arming happens on arrival, and it has already arrived. That is the intent, not a shortcoming.
- It is module state, so `tests/setup.ts` clears it between tests: one test going deaf must not leave the next unable to dwell at all.

- **A mode switch is held only if it moved something under the pointer.** Edit mode brings the language and the voice into the message card, which on some screens grows its row of controls; leaving it shrinks the row and the message box slides up under a pointer still resting where the toggle was. The box's caret dwell then put the caret there — the very start of the words — and everything typed after went in front of what came before. `setMode` notes what a rest would reach (`targetUnderPointer`: the nearest control or field, not the exact node, since a control re-creates its fill as it is pressed), and once the switch is laid out, and again a frame later, `holdIfMovedUnder` holds the dwells until the pointer moves if that is something else. Where nothing moved nothing is held, so a mouse's second click on the same toggle is still a click. jsdom has no `elementFromPoint`, so it never holds there; `e2e/composing.spec.ts` types through the case that scrambled the words.
- **And every control guards itself against arriving under the pointer** (`cameToPointer` in `ui/dwell.ts`), whatever moved it. The guards above are called by whatever moves the screen, so they hold only for the moves somebody remembered to guard — and a list scrolled under a still pointer, a dialog opening where it rests, a row arriving above it, a control replaced by another in its place were each a way to choose something nobody aimed at. So when a control is told the pointer has entered it, it asks where the pointer was before: **the browser reports the entry before the move that caused it**, so the last place seen is still where the pointer came from, and where that is inside the control, the control came to the pointer. It then answers neither a rest nor a click — the click being the dwell, on a gaze rig that sends one — until the pointer leaves or moves the distance that counts as aiming (`MOVED_AWAY_PX`), and a dwell starts from there. **Touch is left alone**: a finger has no place between taps, and every tap is aimed. A control used twice without the pointer leaving is not affected — it had no second arrival. jsdom cannot say what is under a point, so `e2e/click-through.spec.ts` is where it is seen working: a cell scrolled under a resting mouse, refused to a rest and to a click, and answered once the mouse moves across it.

## The pause after choosing

**After anything is accepted, nothing else is for a moment** — a second by default, set in Settings as *Pause after choosing* (`settings.selectionPauseMs`, 0 to 3 seconds, Off at the bottom). The guards above are about the screen moving under a pointer; this one is about the pointer itself. A gaze rests where it last fired and drifts on to whatever is beside it, and a hand, or a rig sending real clicks, can click twice: either way, the next thing chosen inside that second is seldom the thing meant.

- **Kept in `ui/dwell.ts`, beside the guards it joins**, and started in the one place every control reports through as it fires (`noteActivation`), so no control can be missed. `App` hands the setting to it (`setSelectionPause`).
- **A click in the pause is dropped. A dwell begun in it waits, unfilled, and starts filling when the pause ends** if the pointer is still there. Dropping it like a click would leave somebody who moved straight on to their next choice resting on a control that never answers, since arming happens on arrival and they have already arrived. Starting the fill only once the pause is over keeps the fill honest: nothing fills that is not being counted.
- **A control already repeating carries on**, since a held nudge is one choice made over and over rather than a new one. **The keyboard is not held to it**, for the reason it is not held to the screen moving: Enter and Space are aimed by focus, not by where a pointer rests.
- **It travels with the other timings**, to the other devices and into a backup, and the sign-in page is worked with it: it is about somebody's hands and eyes, like the dwell times, not about a screen.
- **Off in the tests, unless a test turns it on** (`withSelectionPause` in `tests/setup.ts`). Nearly every test chooses one thing after another far faster than anybody could with it on, and they are about everything else; `tests/app/pause.test.tsx` and `tests/ui/dwell.test.tsx` are about it, on its real default.
