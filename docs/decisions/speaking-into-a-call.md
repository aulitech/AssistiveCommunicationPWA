# Speaking into a call

> One of the decisions behind Peri, moved out of [AGENTS.md](../../AGENTS.md), which keeps the map of
> the source and the rules that hold everywhere. What is written here is why the code is the shape it is.

**In a video call, nobody heard Peri.** A call sends the microphone, and a call is built to take the sound of the computer's own speakers out of the microphone — echo cancellation, whose whole job is that the people on the other end do not hear themselves come back. Peri's voice comes out of those speakers, so it is exactly what gets removed; with headphones on it never reaches the microphone at all.

**The speaking tab is the way round it** — `speaking/speaking-page.tsx`, served at `/speaking`, and `voice/relay.ts`, which keeps it in step with the board. It shows the last thing said, large, and nothing else, and it **plays** the clip itself. Shared into the call as a tab with its audio — in Google Meet, Present now → A tab → Also share tab audio — the call is sent the tab's sound as a stream of its own, which echo cancellation never touches, and the people on the other end see the words without ever seeing the board.

- **Only sound the tab itself plays is in that stream**, which is why the tab plays the clip rather than Peri. With the tab open, Peri hands it the clip and stays quiet, or the person would hear everything twice. Closed, Peri plays everything itself as it always did.
- **A device voice cannot go this way, and the tab only shows its words.** The synthesiser is played by the operating system rather than by any tab. **Measured, not assumed**: tab capture in Chrome on a Mac carried an `<audio>` clip and none of the device voice — not the Mac's own voices and not Chrome's Google ones — though both spoke. The guide, the menu item and the privacy policy all say so, and an ElevenLabs voice is the way to be heard. Getting a device voice into a call would need Peri to make its own speech in the page, which none of the alternatives below avoids either.
- **Between the tabs is a `BroadcastChannel`**, which reaches the other tabs of this site in this browser and nothing else. Nothing said goes through a server to get from one to the other, so the tab adds nothing to what leaves the device; what the call does with it once it is shared is the call's business, and the privacy policy says that too.
- **A phrase never fails into silence, so a handed clip is answered.** The tab says `playing` or `refused`, and Peri plays it itself on a refusal or after `ANSWER_MS` of nothing — a tab the browser discarded without saying so. That is **the one wait in front of a clip, the emergency bar's included**: bounded, a few milliseconds when the tab is there, and half a second only in the case where the alternative is silence. A tab that did not answer is taken to have gone, so only one clip waits on it. The tab says it has gone when it closes and when the browser freezes it, and that it is here again when it wakes.
- **What the tab shows is what was said**: the words after translation, with their markup off — what `say()` was given, not what the board holds. A voice being tried in the picker is `preview`, said to nobody, and stays out of it.
- **Opened by a click, not by a rest.** A browser opens a tab only in answer to a real click or tap, and the timer a rest fires from is neither. So **Speaking tab** in the menu opens it with `window.open` inside the click — a dwell control fires its action synchronously from a real click — and closes the menu behind it; a rest that is refused leaves the menu open and says so under the item, with the address, rather than doing nothing. **In the menu, not Settings**: it is not a value somebody sets but a place they go, like the guide or Backup & sharing. Opened under one name, `peri-speaking`, so opening it again finds the tab already open.
- **The tab may need one click of its own to play sound**, a browser's rule about pages playing audio. Opened from the menu it did not need one in Chrome; if it does, it says so, and until then Peri plays each clip itself — heard in the room, not in the call — so nothing goes unsaid.
- **Choosing what to share is the call's screen, not Peri's**, and no dwell inside Peri reaches it. Somebody may need to do that part, once a call; the guide says so rather than pretending otherwise.

## What was not done

- **A virtual audio device** — a driver, installed outside the browser, that a call picks as its microphone. A web app cannot install one; it would be a separate installer for each system, a kernel driver on Windows, impossible on an iPad or a Chromebook, it takes the real microphone out of the call, and it still cannot carry a device voice, since the browser offers no way to send built-in speech to a chosen output.
- **A browser extension** feeding Peri's sound into the call's request for the microphone. It would keep the real microphone, but it is a second thing to install and review, and it has the same device-voice gap.
- **Sharing the board's own tab.** It works for an ElevenLabs voice today, and shows the call the board.
