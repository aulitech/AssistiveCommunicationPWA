# Getting started

> One of the decisions behind Peri, moved out of [AGENTS.md](../../AGENTS.md), which keeps the map of
> the source and the rules that hold everywhere. What is written here is why the code is the shape it is.

**Getting started is the guide, one part at a time**, opening when somebody signs in — `menu/introduction.tsx`. A first page says what it is, holds the box that stops it opening at sign-in, and lists every part as an outline with a tick and a date against each one covered. After that it is a part a screen, with Back and Next, at whatever pace the reader keeps.

- **The parts are the guide's own sections**, `HELP_SECTIONS`, in the guide's order, drawn by the same `ProseSections` Menu → Help uses. A second text about how the board works would be a second text to keep in step, and the first time the two disagreed somebody would learn the board from the wrong one. So there is one text and two ways through it, and a change to the guide is a change to Getting started. A new section in the guide is a new part, with nothing else to do.
- **It covers everything because the guide does.** The guide is held to the app by its own tests (`tests/menu/help.test.ts`), and this adds no claims of its own beyond the first page.
- **It opens on signing in, not on every load.** `App` knows when this page signed somebody in, and passes it to `TalkScreen`. A reload in the middle of a conversation is not somebody arriving, and putting a guide in front of a board then would be putting it in front of somebody trying to talk.
- **It stands where the message box, the tabs and the grid are, and the emergency bar stays live under it.** Not a scrim over the whole screen, as the pickers are. Somebody signing in for the first time is exactly who may need the bar before they have read a word.
- **On a wide screen it stands beside the board instead**, the left half, with the board working in the right — `BESIDE_THE_BOARD`, 1200px, where each half is at least 600px: the board's own layout at that width, and a reading column. A part can then be tried as it is read. Narrower, half the screen is room for neither, so it takes the board's place. **Asked in JavaScript** (`ui/media.ts`), not hidden with CSS: on a narrow screen the board is not mounted at all, since a hidden grid measures nothing and renders every cell. Turning a tablet moves between the two as it turns. The wrappers it adds, `.talk-body` and `.board-column`, are `display: contents` except while it is beside the board, so the board's layout is otherwise exactly what it was.
- **One setting, two places to change it**: `Settings.introduction`, on by default, worked by the box on the first page ("Don't show this at sign-in", ticked when it is off) and by the Getting started row in Settings, which can also open it now. It is a setting like the others, so it travels to the user's other devices and is in a backup.
- **A part is covered by moving on from it** — Next, or Finish on the last. Opening one and going back covers nothing. The history is `peri_introduction`, section title to when, under `storageKey` like the rest of a board. Keyed by title because the parts are the sections, so a renamed section starts uncovered, which is right: it says something new. **In no backup and no snapshot**: it is about this person learning the app on this device, which a file handed to somebody else has no use for. The privacy policy lists it.
- **Continue goes to the first part not covered**, so closing it half way and signing in next week picks up where it stopped. Finish goes back to the outline with every tick on it rather than closing, since that is the screen that says it is done, and Start again goes to the first part.
- **Every page arrives under a pointer that has not moved**, the one that signed in and after that the one resting on Next, so each goes deaf for a moment first (`holdDwells`). Closing it does the same for the board it gives back. Outline and Back stay in place and go quiet where they have nowhere to go, rather than coming and going under a resting pointer.
- **The box is not a native checkbox.** A rest raises no click, so it is a dwell control with `role="checkbox"`, like everything else here.
