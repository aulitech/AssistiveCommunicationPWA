# The message card

> One of the decisions behind Peri, moved out of [AGENTS.md](../../AGENTS.md), which keeps the map of
> the source and the rules that hold everywhere. What is written here is why the code is the shape it is.

**The message box and the question box are cards**, shaped like a chat app's input box: the words, and inside the card along the bottom a row of everything that works on them. They replaced a layout in which every control rode one of the box's borders — the modes centred on the upper one, the actions at its right, the slot that emptied the box at the left of the lower one, the edit strip centred on it — which saved the grid a row of height and cost a good deal of positioning arithmetic and a box whose edges were crowded with things painted over them.

- **On top, one row, with the modes centred.** The clipboard at the far left, the modes — microphone, edit, auto-speak, then please and present — in a middle column between two equal ones, so they sit on the card's centre whatever is either side, and the language, the voice and the menu at the right. **There is no Delete in the row any more**: the bin is on the phrase open in the box, against its cell's left edge — see [Editing a phrase](editing-a-phrase.md). It stood at the far right, as far from Save as the card went, its place kept while there was nothing to delete.
- **Under it, the line the words are written on.** The slot that empties the box — Clear, Undo, or in edit mode *start a new phrase* — at its left end, centred on the words however many lines they run to, then the box, with the primary — Speak, or in edit mode Save and then Speak, 1.25rem apart, which says the phrase and saves it — **right after the last word**, filled in the accent as a chat app fills Send and drawn only once there are words. `ui/after-text.tsx` finds where the words end: a never-seen copy of the box holds the same words and then a space the control's width, and the control goes where that space lands — a rem and a few pixels after the last word, so it reads as standing after the words rather than as one more of them, or at the start of the next line, level with the words, when the last has no room, and the box grows to hold that line. The question card's line is the same, with Ask (suggest answers) after the question's last word, filled in the accent as Speak is — it is to the question what Speak is to the message — and the live microphone at the line's right end.
- **Enter and Escape say what the line's two ends say.** Enter in the message box does what the primary does — Speak, or Save in edit mode, and nothing where it would be refused — and Escape what the slot at the line's left end does when there are words: Clear, which Undo puts back, or *start a new phrase*; never Undo itself, so a second Escape does not put back what the first took. **Enter with any modifier is a new line** — Shift, Ctrl, Alt or Command — written through `insertText` in `ui/typing.ts`, since a textarea gives Shift alone. A key held down repeats nothing but the new line. `tests/app/message.test.tsx` holds all of it.
- **A control that moves with every letter is a new hazard for a gaze.** It could land under a pointer resting on the words, and its going — Save, the moment the phrase is saved — leaves the words under a pointer that was resting on it, where a rest places the caret and counts as choosing something (it wiped the mark on the new phrase and the toast saying it was saved). So each time it lands or goes, if the pointer is where it is or was, nothing may be chosen until the pointer moves (`holdIfUnderPointer`); and placed again where it already stood, it has not arrived anywhere. Typing was covered already — the grid narrowing to a typed word holds every dwell — so the browser test drives the case that is not: the screen rewrapping the words under a resting, drifting pointer.
- **The clipboard always keeps the top line's left end.** A narrow card used to put the modes on a line of their own and the clipboard under them, beside the values; now the clipboard stays at the left end of the top line at every width, the modes after it and the menu at the right end — a column kept for it — with the modes centred between the two, and the language and the voice on the line under them. On the narrowest phone the modes wrap, still between the two. The groups are 0.75rem apart there, the gap the modes keep between themselves, which keeps a 360px phone's modes on one line. Asked of the card's own width (`@container message`).
- **The gaps are target-separation numbers.** Every control carries an invisible area 6px past its edges, so two closer than 12px share a rest: 1.25rem between controls, 0.75rem between the modes and between lines. `tests/e2e/layout.spec.ts` measures it at 360, 390 and 1280px, in both modes — every control inside the card, none within 12px of another, the modes on the card's centre.
- **Nothing stands beside the words in edit mode.** A line at the right end of them said what was being edited — *New phrase*, *Editing phrase* — and chose where it was filed, which the box and the tab showing say now; then only that the words were on the board already, which is a toast now, said once as they are written. So the line the words are written on is the same in both modes: the slot that empties it, and the box.
- **The question's line is inside the message box**, in `.message-lines` with the message's: above it on a narrow screen and left of it on a wide one, 2rem apart side by side — a target-separation number — and three-quarters of a rem stacked. It was a card of its own, beside or above the message's, until the cards lost their ground and edge. **Opening or closing it holds the dwells**: either way the message's line moves out from under the pointer that rested on it. See [Settling after the screen moves](settling-after-the-screen-moves.md).
- **The language and the voice have no ground of their own**: the card's shows through them, and the edge alone says each is a control.
- **The menu is in the card, at its upper right** — last in the card's row of controls, after the language, the voice and, in edit mode, Delete, with a gap three times the row's own before it, since it is about the app rather than the message. It was outside the card, in the screen's corner; the card has no ground or edge now, and the menu in its row is still where an app's menu is looked for. On a narrow card, where the row stacks, it stays on the top line, at the right end of the modes'. Back stands in the same corner of every panel the menu opens and holds the dwells as it closes one, so the pointer it leaves over the menu does not open it again. The busy indicator, which stood beside the menu in the corner, is over the lower right of the phrase table now: anywhere in the top row it would cover a control. The keyboard, where it is offered, is at the bar's left end.
- **While anything is being said, Stop covers the whole top row** — the question, the message and the menu — in Clear's muted red taken down far enough for white words (`--stop-red`), with a square and the word *Stop* across the middle. One dwell on it ends the words and whatever was on its way to being said, a translation or a clip included (`stopSpeaking` in `voice/speech.ts`). A sentence going on after somebody has realised it was the wrong one is when they most need a target they cannot miss, so it is the widest thing on the screen. **Laid over the row rather than in place of it** (`StopBar` in `topbar.tsx`): the box keeps its words, its caret and its selection under it, and the row comes back exactly as it was. It arrives under a pointer that rested on Speak, and like every control it answers nothing until that pointer moves — `cameToPointer` — which `e2e/stop.spec.ts` drives in a browser. **And nothing else answers while it is up** but the emergency bar — `setSpeakingLock` in `ui/dwell.ts`, set by `talk.tsx` from `subscribeSpeaking` the moment the words start and stop, so a dwell already filling is stopped with them — and the board is dimmed to say so. Every control asks at the moment it would start or fire, so nothing re-renders for it. The emergency bar stays live because it never waits on anything: saying it stops what was playing. Being arranged or edited, it is a thing to change, and waits like the rest. A voice tried in a picker is not something said, and locks nothing. **How long something is being said** is `subscribeSpeaking`: from the moment it is asked until the utterance or the clip ends, for the latest thing asked only, and a device voice is asked after every quarter second as well as listened to, since a synthesiser that is cancelled or fails does not always say so.
- **Clear is a heavy X in a muted red** (`--clear-red`, the danger red let down towards the grey): it throws words away, but less finally than the bin, which is the full red.
- **Focus is always in a box**: the question's while it is open, the message's otherwise — `focusBox` in `topbar.tsx`. Put there as the question opens and as it closes, and put back whenever focus falls to nothing (a panel or dialog closing, a press on empty space), so a key typed always lands in one of the two; the message box gets back the caret its own state says it had, Chrome having moved an unfocused box's caret to the start without saying so. Focus moved elsewhere by Tab, or into another field, is left there. No control takes focus on a press either — see `keepFocus` in `ui/dwell.ts`.
- **The menu is a bare glyph**, with no ground or edge of its own, like the controls in the cards.
- **The box has the ground, and nothing round it does.** The card — the row of controls and the line under it — has no ground, edge or shadow of its own; the message box has the ground (`--card-ground`, the darkest that keeps the muted grey at 7:1) and rounded corners, so what reads as the place to write is where the words go. The card had the ground and the edge and said focus with `:focus-within`; with no edge to say it on, each box says focus with a line along its foot, drawn inside it (`--card-focus`). Edit mode dashes the message box's edge in the accent, as an outline inside it. The question box has neither ground nor edge.
- **Every control in either card is the microphone's size**, 2rem with a 1.2rem glyph, so the row reads as one set. `tests/app/message.test.tsx` holds it.
- **Rest is the open space in the card's row of controls**, not a control in it. It was a bar among the modes, half a rem tall with an invisible area round it a tracker could hit — the smallest thing in the row, for the one control that has to be found when nothing else answers. Now a surface behind everything in that row takes the pointer wherever nothing in it answers: its groups pass it through (`pointer-events`), and its controls keep it. **Only that row** — it was the whole top row for a while, the padding round the cards included, which put a rest that switches everything off wherever a gaze happened to settle between the box and the menu. It draws nothing of its own until it is rested on. A dwell or a click in the gaps between the clipboard, the modes and the values switches dwelling off. While it is off, **Resume covers that row** (`ResumeBar`) — opaque, the brand's gold a little muted, the word near-black, since white on that gold was under 2:1 — as Stop covers the top row while anything is said, and is the one control that answers. **After either, neither answers until the pointer has left the row** (`mustLeave` in `Topbar`): each comes up where the other was, under a pointer that has not moved, and the guard every control keeps asks only for a small move, not for leaving; **Paused** stands large in the middle of the screen, letting the pointer through to what is under it; and everything round it is dimmed because none of it will answer — dimmed piece by piece, since a row inside something dimmed would be dimmed with it. `RestSurface` in `talk/topbar.tsx`.
