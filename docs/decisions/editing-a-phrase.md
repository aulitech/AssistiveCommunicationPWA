# Editing a phrase

> One of the decisions behind Peri, moved out of [AGENTS.md](../../AGENTS.md), which keeps the map of
> the source and the rules that hold everywhere. What is written here is why the code is the shape it is.

**In edit mode the language and the voice in the card's row are the phrase's, not the board's** — the voice it is said in, and the language that voice is for. There is no second voice picker for that reason: one pair of controls meaning whichever of the two the mode says, rather than two pairs that look alike and are not. The language is still the board's, because a phrase's voice is remembered *per* language and that is the one the lookup will use. **The pair is drawn in edit mode at any width**, unlike outside it, where it is gated on `WIDE_ENOUGH` to avoid building the device's voice list on a phone that never shows it: this is the only place a phrase can be given a voice now, and a control that exists on a monitor and not on a phone is a feature somebody cannot reach.

**In edit mode the message box is the phrase editor.** There was a dialog for it, and a dialog is the wrong shape for a board driven by gaze: it covered the very phrases being edited, it had to be got out of before anything else could be reached, and every one of its controls was a target somewhere the pointer had never been. So there is always a draft — `talk/use-editor.ts` — and choosing a phrase points the draft at it rather than opening anything.

- **The rail changes with the mode, in the same places.** Speak becomes save, and copy goes; Clear becomes "start a new phrase". Paste is the one that means the same thing either way, which is why it keeps its place while the others change.
- **The phrase open in the box carries its own tools, as the tab showing does** — `ItemTool`, flush with the right edge of its cell, or of its button on the emergency bar: **the bin against its left edge and the drag pad against its right**, each glyph by its edge with 1.5rem of the tool between it and the words, both on the cell's ground two shades lighter (a wash of white, so the emergency bar's blue lightens as the grid's grey does). The pad lifts it — see [Ordering the grid](ordering-the-grid.md#arranging-a-category-by-hand) and [The emergency bar](the-emergency-bar.md) — and is not there where the phrase cannot be moved: the board narrowed by what is typed, Translations, the answers, a phrase pinned to a tab that does not hold it. The bin was once the pad's, coming in its place when the words changed as the tab's does while it is renamed; it is there from the start now, on request. **Every bin asks first**, `AskFirst` in `editors.tsx`, saying what it will do here — *Forget this translation?*, *Take it out of Food?*, *Delete this phrase?* — with the confirm at the far end. **And the question holds everything until it is answered**: neither answer takes a rest or a click until the pointer has gone 120px from where the bin was chosen (`ASK_FIRST_MOVE_PX`, five times the ordinary measure of a pointer aimed somewhere new — a gaze resting where the bin was, or drifting off it, must not answer the question it raised), and the hold outlasts the question if the pointer has not gone. **An answer rested on starts filling the moment the pointer has gone far enough**, without leaving it first (`armsOnceMoved` in `ui/dwell.ts`): everything else arms only on arriving, and *Keep it*, the nearer answer, was usually reached before the pointer had gone the distance — refused then, it never filled however long it was rested on. Both answers fill along their foot as they are rested on, which they never did. Every key is taken before anything else sees it: Enter confirms and Escape keeps it, from the start, and nothing is typed, pasted or dropped anywhere. It is portalled over everything, Peri's keyboard and the emergency bar included, and touching the screen round it is not an answer. **The emergency bar is behind it too**, which is the one place the app makes it wait — on a dwell on *Keep it*, after a decision somebody asked to make. The bin stood in the card's row at the far right, as far from Save as the card went, its place kept while there was nothing to delete; it went there because the tools are on the thing they act on everywhere else. **The open phrase stays on the board once it is being changed**: typing after it searches Library, and a search that left it out would take its cell and its bin with it, so it is put first where the tab or the search does not show it (`gridPhrases` in `talk.tsx`) — never before, since turning edit mode on must not move a cell under a resting pointer.
- **Nothing says what is being edited** — *New phrase*, *Editing phrase*, *Keep this translation* stood at the right end of the line all the time, saying what the box with the words in it says already. Words already on the board are said in a toast — see below. **Where it is filed is not chosen there**: Save puts it in the category whose tab is showing as Save is pressed, or Library alone on Library or a record — `into` in `talk/use-editor.ts`. A category trigger stood there, onto a full-screen grid of categories with Done and Cancel, and it was one more trip for every phrase written for a choice the tab already makes. Its voice is the pair in the card's row, as it was.
- **The same wording is never made twice.** A second copy is a cell somebody has to read past to reach the one they meant. So where the words are on the board already, **Save puts that phrase in the category showing** rather than copying it, and a toast says so as they are written — *Already on the board — Save adds it to Pantry* — and anything opened in the box is left as it was. Where there is nothing for it to do — the category showing has it already, or the tab is Library — Save goes quiet and the toast says why, *Already in Kitchen* or *Already on the board*: a control that has gone quiet explains nothing by itself. Matched on the source, folded to lower case with its spaces collapsed; `duplicateOf` in `use-board.ts` keys the board once and answers in constant time; a phrase does not duplicate itself, or opening one to fix a typo could never be saved.
- **A deleted phrase can be put back.** Deleting was the one change the app offered no way back from, with the bin then a dwell's breadth from Save. The slot at the box's lower left — *start a new phrase*, which has nothing to do the moment after a delete, the bin having left the draft blank — becomes **Undo deleting "…"**, naming it, with the composer's undo glyph since it means the same thing. `removePhrase` hands back a `Removed`: the phrase's own entry (or, for one Peri ships, the fact that it was hidden) and its places on the emergency bar and in its category's arrangement; its wording, voice and any category it was moved to never leave the store, which is what lets that be so small. `forgetUsed` hands back the count it dropped. Undo puts all of it back **where it was**, opens the phrase in the box — where it was when the bin took it — and marks it. **One step, and short-lived**: anything written or opened after the delete takes the offer away (adjusted during render, so no frame offers an undo that would write over a draft), as does leaving edit mode. Forgetting a sent message offers nothing: that takes something off a record, not off the board.
- **A category can be deleted with its phrases in it, and the dialog asks first.** Delete was offered only on an empty category, which left emptying one — a dwell on each phrase, then the bin — as the only way to be rid of it. It is the bin on the tab showing, which stands where the reorder tool was — the only control on the tab — while the name is being changed, and it only asks: *Delete Food?*, saying its phrases stay in Library — see [Categories as references](categories-as-references.md). **The delete that confirms sits at the far end of the dialog's row, and the way back nearer** — nothing promises what lines up with the bin, only that nothing under a resting pointer deletes — and nothing arms until the pointer has moved (`holdDwellsUntilMoved`), so a gaze still resting on the bin cannot answer the question it raised. `removeCategory` in `use-board.ts` takes the category and its references and **deletes no phrase**: a category only ever referred to Library's. A phrase open in the box, or new words ticked into it, simply drop the category from the ones they are in. **No undo**: the question is the guard, and it is an order that goes, not anything anybody said.
- **A category goes with its last phrase, and an import leaves no empty one behind.** A category somebody makes is kept while it is empty, so one can be made first and filled afterwards — and that same rule used to keep it for ever once everything in it had been deleted or moved out, a tab opening onto a blank grid. `withoutEmptyCategories` in `core/board.ts` takes the ones that refer to nothing left on the board, with their place in the hand-made order. **A removal, a delete or a move drops only the category it emptied**, so one made a moment ago and not yet filled survives a phrase going somewhere else; **an import — a backup or a sheet — drops every empty one**, since a file somebody else made can name categories this board has nothing in, and replacing can empty the ones it had. **What another device sends is left alone**: a category made there a moment ago is theirs to fill, and `restore` only prunes when it is told the store came from an import. **Undoing puts the category back where it stood** among the tabs, and the phrase back in it, since `Removed` carries both.
- **Saving closes nothing, because nothing was opened.** The draft goes back to blank and a toast says what happened — without it a save has no visible result beyond one cell appearing somewhere among a couple of thousand.
- **A new phrase is filed where somebody is**, so the board stays: under the tab showing, or in Library, which holds every phrase. **The one move left is keeping a record** — a translation or an answer kept on its own tab goes to Library, where it is, the record it came off being left exactly as it was. The move holds the dwells (`holdDwellsUntilMoved`) because every cell has just been replaced under a pointer that has not moved and in edit mode the one landing underneath opens for rewording; a save that leaves somebody where they were holds nothing, having replaced nothing.
- **And it shows them where it went**, in three parts, because a save that leaves nothing on screen but a toast is a save with nothing to show for it. The new cell takes **the mark the last phrase chosen takes** — one mark meaning *that one, and it went there*, rather than a second one to learn, and it goes the same way, on the next dwell whatever that is. The grid **brings the cell into view**, centred, a cell against the top edge sitting under the message box and one against the bottom under the emergency bar. And the bar **brings the tab to its middle**, but only where the board moved by itself (`pointing` on `FilterBar`): a board with many categories is several screens of tabs, while a tab somebody dwelled on is one they were already looking at, and sliding that out from under a gaze that has not moved is how the next dwell lands on its neighbour. `pointing` in `talk.tsx` carries both halves. Two things make the scroll work rather than quietly do nothing: the cell carries `data-phrase`, since the list is rearranged under it by four orders, and **the render window is grown to reach it** — it starts again at a screenful on arriving at a tab and a category runs to a hundred and eighty phrases, so the new cell is often past the end of what is rendered, and a cell that does not exist is nothing to scroll to. Both are adjusted during the render that names the phrase, which is why the scroll can ask once rather than watch the window.
- **A message box already holding a phrase opens that phrase, and nothing else moves.** Choosing a phrase in auto-speak puts its words in the box, so a box holding exactly one phrase is somebody pointing at that phrase — and what reaching for edit mode gave them was a copy of it that could not be saved and said only *Already on the board*. So `phraseSaying` in `use-board.ts` is asked — Library holds each wording once, so there is one phrase to find — and **the editor opens on the phrase rather than on a copy of it**: the categories it is in and its voice are what the strip shows, Save saves it and the bin deletes it. The match is on the **source**, as the duplicate guard matches, so a phrase with a slot in it never matches — what is in the box is one filling of it. **The tab, the grid and the box stay as they were**: the board used to go to Library, mark the phrase and scroll to it, which put the cell somebody had just been looking at somewhere else the moment the mode came on. Turning the mode on changes what a dwell does, not what is in front of them. The toast says what was opened and that it is in Library.
- **Switching modes changes nothing in the box or the grid.** Entering edit mode carries the message in as a new phrase, so "keep what I just wrote" is a mode switch rather than a feature; **leaving it, what the box holds becomes the message** — the words, the caret and what was typed, through `load` in `use-composer.ts` — so the grid stays narrowed to the same word. A phrase with choices in it is carried as the board shows it, since a message says what is in it and `{drink}` would be said as written; the message that was there before goes to the composer's history. **A phrase auto-speak says goes into the box too, in place of what was there and selected** (`showSaid` in `use-composer.ts`) — so it can be copied, said again with Speak, or typed over, and the next key written replaces it. What the box held is not kept for Undo: it was most often what was typed to find the phrase, and Undo brings back only the last thing cleared. It is put there as a phrase rather than as typing, so the board does not narrow to its words, and a phrase chosen once edit mode is on goes after it rather than before it — the caret is read from the end of a selection. A phrase just said is not a message being written, so a question in listen mode still answers itself over it (`holdsSaid`). **It is the phrase as it was written** (`composeWritten`) — markup, the full stop at its end — **and so is a phrase built into a message**. **A phrase with choices goes in as edit mode shows it, choices and all** (its `source`), and they are asked for when it is said: in auto-speak the picker opens as it is chosen and the words chosen are said, and building a message, **Speak asks** — `handleSpeak` in `talk.tsx` reads the box as the board reads a phrase, the user's own lists in it (`aliasOverlay`), and a box holding choices opens the same picker over the whole message and says what was chosen. The box keeps the choices, so the next Speak asks again. Filled in as it went in, the box held one wording of the phrase: somebody who wanted the other word chose the phrase again, and edit mode could not open what the box held as the phrase it came from: in every mode the box holds the words edit mode opens the phrase on, rather than the version cut down for speaking. Building a message put the phrase in without its full stop for most of the app's life, so the box said one thing and edit mode another, and turning edit mode on over it opened a copy that could not be saved rather than the phrase. **`speak` takes the full stop at the end off again**, as `compose` does, so a phrase said from the box is the words the board says — the same shipped translation, the same entry under Translations, the same clip paid for — and `isFilledFrom` does not count it, so a phrase with its choices made is still the phrase it came from. **And Speak says it again as it was said**, while the box holds it untouched: in the phrase's own voice, and a cell off Translations in the language it already is, neither sent to be translated a second time nor kept a second time. **Selected in the commit that writes it**: it was selected on a timer set beside the write, and a dwell — which fires from a timer of its own — could render after it, so a phrase chosen by resting was selected one time and not the next. Leaving edit mode used to give back the message as it was before, which changed the box under somebody who had been writing in it; it was changed on request. The microphone does not carry the draft, since opening the question empties the box anyway.
- **What is typed searches the board in edit mode, as it does everywhere else.** One rule, `talk/typed.ts`, for the message and for the phrase being written: what was put in the box — a phrase chosen, a phrase opened to edit, a message carried in — is finished and narrows nothing, and **what is typed after it searches Library**, up to the caret. So opening a phrase off a narrowed board lets the whole tab back (holding the dwells, every cell having changed under the pointer that chose it), turning edit mode on carries the word being typed in with the message and changes nothing in the grid, and *start a new phrase* starts with the whole tab. Edit mode used to leave the grid alone as the draft was typed, so as not to take away the phrases somebody came to edit; it was changed on request, so a search works the same way in every mode — the phrase to edit is found by typing it. While the grid is narrowed the open phrase carries its bin rather than its drag pad, since arranging would arrange only what matched. `tests/app/editing.test.tsx` drives it, and `tests/app/message.test.tsx` through Peri's own keys.
- **Edit mode is drawn on the box's own edge**, dashed and accent-tinted — an outline drawn inside the box rather than a border, so the box keeps its size and the words do not rewrap as the mode changes; focus brightens it to the accent. It was the card's edge until the card lost its ground and edge.
- **The board opens talking, and with the caret in the box.** Auto-speak is the state every load starts in, and the message box carries `autoFocus` so somebody with a keyboard can type the first thing they want to say without having to place the caret first — the one thing a dwell could not do at all until `useCaretDwell`. A programmatic focus does not raise a phone's on-screen keyboard, which needs a real gesture.
- **Two ways of working, and always exactly one.** A dwell on a phrase either says it (**auto-speak**) or brings it into the box (**edit**): into an empty box it opens, to be reworded or deleted, and into one with words in it, it goes in at the caret (`choose` in `use-editor.ts`, through `insertAt` in `talk/typed.ts`), as edit mode shows it — choices and all, and a blank selected so the word typed next fills it. **Turning one off turns the other on.** There was a third, **composing**, where a phrase went into the box and nothing else happened, and the two toggles each meant their own state with neither on meaning that one; it was taken out on request, and a sentence built from phrases is built in edit mode's box now. So **Speak sits after Save in edit mode and saves as well** — `handleSpeak` in `talk.tsx` says the phrase being written, in the voice chosen for it and its choices asked for as anywhere else, and saves it as Save would; where Save would refuse because Library holds the words already, it only says them. Choosing in edit mode **holds the dwells until the pointer moves**, opened or put in, since the box changes under the pointer that chose and can push the grid down — which choosing a phrase to build a message always did. A phrase put in is counted as used, as one said is; one opened to be reworded is not. To reword a different phrase, *start a new phrase* empties the box first. **And nothing is chosen off the board while the box holds a phrase as it was opened** (`choosing` in `talk.tsx`): choosing into words puts a phrase in at the caret, so a rest meant only for reading the board put a second phrase into the first. The grid's cells are dimmed, answer nothing (`is-inert`) and are not magnified until the words change — then somebody is building something and a phrase goes in as before — though they are still somewhere to drop a phrase in the air. **The emergency bar is left alone, and speaks**: it is the one thing on the screen that has to answer whatever else is going on, so while the box holds a phrase or any words, a button on it chosen in edit mode is said, as it is in auto-speak, rather than put into them (`saysInEditMode`) — somebody in the middle of rewording something who needs help has no time to clear the box first. Into an empty box it opens, which is how an emergency phrase is changed. An empty box opens what is chosen. Auto-speak is where the board ships, so opening Peri for the first time is enough to say something. `setMode` in `talk.tsx` is the only thing that writes any of it, and **the microphone writes it too** — see [Listen mode](listen-mode.md).

`Draft` derives everything it can rather than copying it: `text`, `category` and `voice` are stored only once touched, and everything else is read off the phrase each render, so a draft cannot go stale against a store that changed underneath it.
