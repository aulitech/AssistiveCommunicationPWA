# Editing a phrase

> One of the decisions behind Peri, moved out of [AGENTS.md](../../AGENTS.md), which keeps the map of
> the source and the rules that hold everywhere. What is written here is why the code is the shape it is.

**In edit mode the two controls at the box's lower-right corner are the phrase's, not the board's** — the voice it is said in, and the language that voice is for. `PhraseEditBar` carries no voice picker of its own for that reason: one pair of controls meaning whichever of the two the mode says, rather than two pairs that look alike and are not. The language is still the board's, because a phrase's voice is remembered *per* language and that is the one the lookup will use. **The pair is drawn in edit mode at any width**, unlike outside it, where it is gated on `WIDE_ENOUGH` to avoid building the device's voice list on a phone that never shows it: this is the only place a phrase can be given a voice now, and a control that exists on a monitor and not on a phone is a feature somebody cannot reach.

**In edit mode the message box is the phrase editor.** There was a dialog for it, and a dialog is the wrong shape for a board driven by gaze: it covered the very phrases being edited, it had to be got out of before anything else could be reached, and every one of its controls was a target somewhere the pointer had never been. So there is always a draft — `talk/use-editor.ts` — and choosing a phrase points the draft at it rather than opening anything.

- **The rail changes with the mode, in the same three places.** Speak, copy and paste become save, delete and paste; Clear becomes "start a new phrase". Paste is the one that means the same thing either way, which is why it keeps the end of the row while the two beside it change. Delete **goes quiet rather than away** while there is nothing to delete, for the reason the emergency bar's add control does: a control that comes and goes moves the ones beside it, and these are aimed at rather than read.
- **A second strip rides the box's lower border**, centred on it exactly as the modes ride the upper one, and carrying the two things a phrase has besides its words — the category and the voice. Both are triggers onto a full-screen grid, because that is the only shape of "one out of many" a dwell can work. The category was a native `<select>`, whose list the operating system draws outside the page, where nothing can be hovered at all. Its grid ends in a tile that is not a category: **New category…** hands over to the same `CategoryModal` the tabs use, since naming one is a keyboard job. **A tile marks a category and Done files the phrase there** — `usePendingChoice`, as the other grids do. It wrote each tile into the draft as it was rested on and put the first back on Cancel, so looking at the grid and leaving it marked the draft as started: *Start a new phrase* woke with nothing to start again from, an offer to undo a delete went away, and a blank draft stopped following the tab. **The two are one number**, `--edit-bar-inset`: it is `.topbar` padding-bottom in edit mode, which is where the box's lower border falls, and it is the strip's own `bottom`, which with `translateY(50%)` is where the strip's centre line falls. It is half the strip's stated height, so the lower edge reaches the bottom of the bar and no further. Written as two numbers they drifted apart at the first change — the padding grew and the strip stayed put, leaving it centred 20px *below* the border it rides.
- **The same wording cannot be filed twice in one category.** A second copy is a cell somebody has to read past to reach the one they meant, so Save goes quiet and the strip says *Already on the board* — a control that has gone quiet explains nothing by itself. Matched on the source, folded to lower case with its spaces collapsed, and **only within a category**: the table lists "Good morning" under Interpersonal, Texting and Time of Day on purpose, because somebody looks in whichever of the three they think in. `duplicateOf` in `use-board.ts` keys the board once and answers in constant time; a phrase does not duplicate itself, or opening one to fix a typo could never be saved.
- **A deleted phrase can be put back.** Deleting was the one change the app offered no way back from, with the bin a dwell's breadth from Save. The slot at the box's lower left — *start a new phrase*, which has nothing to do the moment after a delete, the bin having left the draft blank — becomes **Undo deleting "…"**, naming it, with the composer's undo glyph since it means the same thing. `removePhrase` hands back a `Removed`: the phrase's own entry (or, for one Peri ships, the fact that it was hidden) and its places on the emergency bar and in its category's arrangement; its wording, voice and any category it was moved to never leave the store, which is what lets that be so small. `forgetUsed` hands back the count it dropped. Undo puts all of it back **where it was**, opens the phrase in the box — where it was when the bin took it — and marks it. **One step, and short-lived**: anything written or opened after the delete takes the offer away (adjusted during render, so no frame offers an undo that would write over a draft), as does leaving edit mode. Forgetting a sent message offers nothing: that takes something off a record, not off the board.
- **A category can be deleted with its phrases in it, and the dialog asks first.** Delete was offered only on an empty category, which left emptying one — a dwell on each phrase, then the bin — as the only way to be rid of it. It is in the rename dialog for every category now, and it only asks: the dialog becomes *Delete Food?*, with how many phrases go with it. **The delete that confirms sits at the far end of the row, where Save was, and the way back at Delete's end** — the dialog is shorter without its name field and re-centres, so what the rows line up with is not promised, only that nothing under a resting pointer deletes — and nothing arms until the pointer has moved (`holdDwellsUntilMoved`) — a gaze still resting on the first Delete cannot answer the question it raised. `removeCategory` in `use-board.ts` takes each phrase the way a single delete does, one somebody wrote removed and one Peri ships hidden, and **forgets a rename or a move that pointed at the category**, or one made later under the same name would inherit it. A phrase open in the box that has just gone is let go of; new words filed there by hand keep their text and find another home. **No undo**: the question is the guard, and undoing a category's worth is not the one-step offer the lower-left slot makes.
- **A category goes with its last phrase, and an import leaves no empty one behind.** A category somebody makes is kept while it is empty, so one can be made first and filled afterwards — and that same rule used to keep it for ever once everything in it had been deleted or moved out, a tab opening onto a blank grid. `withoutEmptyCategories` in `core/board.ts` takes the ones with nothing left in them, with their place in the hand-made order and their arrangement. **A delete or a move drops only the category it emptied**, so one made a moment ago and not yet filled survives a phrase going somewhere else; **an import — a backup or a sheet — drops every empty one**, since a file somebody else made can name categories this board has nothing in, and replacing can empty the ones it had. **What another device sends is left alone**: a category made there a moment ago is theirs to fill, and `restore` only prunes when it is told the store came from an import. **Undoing the delete puts the category back where it stood**, in both lists, since `Removed` carries where it was.
- **Saving closes nothing, because nothing was opened.** The draft goes back to blank and a toast says what happened — without it a save has no visible result beyond one cell appearing somewhere among a couple of thousand.
- **A new phrase takes the board to where it was filed.** Anywhere but the tab in front of them it lands among a couple of thousand cells, and all somebody has to show for writing one is a toast that fades. Three cases do not move, and none is an exception to that: on **All** the phrase is already on screen, the **emergency bar** is on screen under every tab, and a phrase being **reworded** is one somebody is moving *out* of where they are — refiling several out of one category is a run they would be thrown out of after the first. **Keeping a message is the case with the least to show for it otherwise**, the record it came off being left exactly as it was. The move holds the dwells (`holdDwellsUntilMoved`) because every cell has just been replaced under a pointer that has not moved and in edit mode the one landing underneath opens for rewording; a save that leaves somebody where they were holds nothing, having replaced nothing.
- **And it shows them where it went**, in three parts, because a save that leaves nothing on screen but a toast is a save with nothing to show for it. The new cell takes **the mark the last phrase chosen takes** — one mark meaning *that one, and it went there*, rather than a second one to learn, and it goes the same way, on the next dwell whatever that is. The grid **brings the cell into view**, centred, a cell against the top edge sitting under the message box and one against the bottom under the emergency bar. And the bar **brings the tab to its middle**, but only where the board moved by itself (`pointing` on `FilterBar`): forty-two categories is several screens of tabs, while a tab somebody dwelled on is one they were already looking at, and sliding that out from under a gaze that has not moved is how the next dwell lands on its neighbour. `pointing` in `talk.tsx` carries both halves. Two things make the scroll work rather than quietly do nothing: the cell carries `data-phrase`, since the list is rearranged under it by four orders, and **the render window is grown to reach it** — it starts again at a screenful on arriving at a tab and a category runs to a hundred and eighty phrases, so the new cell is often past the end of what is rendered, and a cell that does not exist is nothing to scroll to. Both are adjusted during the render that names the phrase, which is why the scroll can ask once rather than watch the window.
- **A message box already holding a phrase takes the board to it.** Choosing a phrase while composing puts its words in the box, so a box holding exactly one phrase is somebody pointing at that phrase — and what reaching for edit mode gave them was a copy of it, filed wherever they happened to be standing, that could not be saved and said only *Already on the board*. Now `phraseSaying` in `use-board.ts` is asked, and the board goes to that phrase's category, marks the cell and brings it into view, the same `pointing` a new phrase uses. **Off All as well**, unlike a phrase just saved, and the difference is the question: a save asks *did it go, and where*, which the toast answers and All was already showing; this asks *which category is this in*, and All is the one tab that cannot answer it. **All is the commonest case**, being where the board opens and where a phrase is most often chosen without knowing its category — but it goes from any tab the phrase is not on. Where the same wording is filed under several categories the copy on the tab in front of them wins, that being the one they are looking at; `wordingKey` in `core/store.ts` is `phraseKey` without the category, which is the half that question needs. **And the editor opens on the phrase rather than on a copy of it**: the box holds what the phrase was written as, its own category and voice are what the strip shows, Save saves it and the bin deletes it. Handing over a copy cost a dwell spent finding again the cell the board had just finished pointing at — and the copy could not be saved at all, being a duplicate of the phrase it was a copy of. The toast says what was opened and which category it went to, since both the box's contents and the tab have changed under somebody. The match is on the **source**, as the duplicate guard matches, so a phrase with a slot in it never matches — what is in the box is one filling of it.
- **Entering edit mode on a category files the next new phrase under it.** Somebody who switches to Greetings and then reaches for edit mode is adding to Greetings, nearly every time, and what they got instead was a picker landing on wherever the last phrase went — which after one trip to another tab is the wrong answer for every phrase after it, each costing a dwell on the trigger, a dwell on a tile and a dwell on Done to put right. It is the same `peri_recent` a save writes, through `fileUnder` in `talk.tsx`, so there is one answer to *where does a new phrase go* rather than two. **Only where the tab is a category**: All is every category at once and Sent, Answers and Translations are records of what was said, so none of the four is somewhere a phrase can be filed and each leaves the last choice standing rather than throwing it away and making somebody pick again. `allCategories.includes` is the whole of that test, those four being the only tabs that are not in it. **And on arriving at one in edit mode, while the draft is blank**: a tab goes to its category in edit mode now, and somebody moving between tabs before writing anything is looking for where the next phrase goes — `chooseTab` in `talk.tsx`. A draft with anything in it stays where it is, words written or a category picked, or refiling it would move a phrase somebody had already started.
- **The message and the draft are separate.** Entering edit mode carries the message in as a new phrase, so "keep what I just wrote" is a mode switch rather than a feature — but writing a phrase never writes the message, and leaving edit mode gives it back untouched.
- **The grid does not narrow to the draft.** `typed` searches Library while a message is being composed; in edit mode `filterWord` is empty, or the phrases somebody came to edit would disappear as they typed. That holds however the letters arrive — including from Peri's own keyboard, where narrowing is most of the point. `tests/app/message.test.tsx` drives all three modes through the keys. The guard only bites when edit mode was entered with something already composed, which is the only way to test it: writing a phrase writes the draft and never the message, so a test that clears the box first passes with the guard taken out.
- **Edit mode is drawn on the box's own border**, dashed and accent-tinted, rather than as an outline over it. A textarea already has a border and an outline sits just outside one, so the two read as a double line around a single box; the focus colour is restated at the same specificity so it still shows through.
- **The board opens talking, and with the caret in the box.** Auto-speak is the state every load starts in, and the message box carries `autoFocus` so somebody with a keyboard can type the first thing they want to say without having to place the caret first — the one thing a dwell could not do at all until `useCaretDwell`. A programmatic focus does not raise a phone's on-screen keyboard, which needs a real gesture.
- **Three states, two controls, and each control means its own state.** A dwell on a phrase either says it (**auto-speak**), collects it (**composing**), or opens it (**edit**), and never more than one of those. Auto-speak is where the board ships, so opening Peri for the first time is enough to say something. **Switching one on switches the other off; switching one off leaves the other alone** — speaking and rewording ask opposite things of the same dwell, so the board cannot be in both, but *not speaking* is not a request to start rewording. They sat in a ring instead, where auto-speak off landed in edit mode: the one toggle somebody building a sentence reaches for put them in the one mode where a dwell changes the board, and composing, which is what they were after, was two dwells away — the furthest apart pair in the ring was the pair used most. Every state is one dwell from the other two now, and neither control ever does nothing. `setMode` in `talk.tsx` is the only thing that writes any of it, and **the microphone writes it too** — see [Listen mode](listen-mode.md).

`Draft` derives everything it can rather than copying it: `text`, `category` and `voice` are stored only once touched, and everything else is read off the phrase each render, so a draft cannot go stale against a store that changed underneath it.
