# One Library

**Every phrase Peri ships is filed under Library, and each wording is there once.** They came in forty-two categories — Idioms, Food, Texting, Responses — and the same words were filed in two, three and four of them: *Good morning* under Interpersonal, Texting and Time of Day. Across the table 2,447 rows held 2,217 wordings, and every repeat is a cell a gaze has to read past to reach the one it meant. The categories somebody makes are theirs; the ones Peri shipped were a guess about how everybody thinks, which is the part of the board least worth defending.

- **A duplicate is two rows with the same words**, compared as the board draws them, without regard to case or punctuation — so *No Way* and *No way*, *What is next ?* and *What is next?*, *I'll  let you know* with its double space, and *How are you* and *How are you?* are one each. A blank is part of the wording, so *How will I ___?* is not *How will I*. **Of two that differ in punctuation, the punctuated one stays** — the question mark is how a voice knows to ask rather than tell, and the comma in *Come, talk with me* is a pause somebody wrote on purpose. Fifteen pairs went that way, most of them a texting expansion (*How are you*, what HRU stands for) beside the question it abbreviates; `core/texting.test.ts` holds each acronym to the punctuated one now. Otherwise the copy kept is the first in the table, unless another is better written (fewer capitals, no stray space before a mark). **They were settled in two passes**, the second folding each unpunctuated copy into its twin by the same `merged` list as the first, so a copy dropped either time is in `FORMER_IDS`.
- **Every phrase keeps the id it had.** An id is the category and the words together (`phraseId`), and everything somebody has done to a phrase is written against it — its wording, its voice, hiding it, where they moved it, how often it is used — on this device, in their backups, and on their other devices, some of which will be on an older release for a while. Made from Library, every one of those would have come loose at once. So each row carries **`was`**, the category it came in, and its id is still made from that. A phrase added to the table from now on has no `was` and is made from Library.
- **The copies that went are listed on the phrase they were folded into**, as **`merged`** — each as `category|words`, which is what its id was made from. `FORMER_IDS` in `core/phrases.ts` is that list as ids: the id each dropped copy had, and the id of the phrase it became.
- **`foldFormerCopies` moves everything written against a dropped copy onto the phrase that stayed**, and every source of a store goes through it — storage in `loadPhraseStore`, and a backup or another device in `useBoard`'s `restore`. The phrase's own entry wins where both have one; what only a copy had comes across, so a copy somebody reworded or gave a voice is not undone. A voice is per language, so the two are merged language by language. Arrangements name the phrase once. **Usage is folded where it is read**, in `loadUsage`: a copy's count is added to the phrase's, and the later of the two times is kept. **A sheet's IDs are folded in `applySheet`**, so a spreadsheet saved before is matched rather than added again.
- **Hiding needs to know when.** Somebody who deleted one copy of a phrase and kept another still had it on their board, and the collapse must not take it away — so the first time a board written before is read, a phrase comes back if any copy of it was showing. Only that once: afterwards it is hidden because they hid it. **A store records which table it was written against** — `table: 2`, stamped by `savePhraseStore` rather than held in `PhraseStore`, since nothing else has any use for it — and a store without the stamp is one this is the first look at. Until the board is next saved it is read the same way each time, so the answer does not change under anybody. A backup or another device does not bring anything back: a copy it hid is dropped, and the phrase is hidden only if the phrase itself was.
- **The categories that went are simply gone.** A rename of one (*Food* → *Meals*) names nothing now; a phrase somebody moved *into* one keeps its place, and the category stays as long as something is in it. The texting acronyms are found by typing them, as they always were — `core/texting.test.ts` reads which phrases came from Texting off `was` and `merged`.
- **This was a major release**: the categories somebody relied on are gone. A backup or a sheet from before still reads, and an older device still understands every id a newer one writes, since none of them changed.
